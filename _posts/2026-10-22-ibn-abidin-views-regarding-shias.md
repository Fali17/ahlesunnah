Ibn ʿĀbidīn discusses this issue in Radd al-Muḥtār and further explains it in his Tanbīh al-Wulāt wa-l-Ḥukkām. The point is that there are different statements transmitted in the books of the madhhab, and he does not consider every statement of takfīr found in a book of fatwā to be the position that should be followed.

He first mentions what was transmitted in al-Khulāṣah, through al-Bazzāziyyah, that a Rāfiḍī who curses the two Shaykhs, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with them), is a disbeliever, whereas one who merely prefers ʿAlī over them is an innovator. He then comments:

“This does not entail that repentance is not accepted. Moreover, declaring him a disbeliever is problematic, in view of what is stated in al-Ikhtiyār: the imams agreed upon declaring the people of innovation misguided and regarding them as mistaken, and that insulting or hating any of the Companions does not constitute disbelief; rather, the person is declared misguided.”

(Radd al-Muḥtār, 4:237, Ḥalabī edition.)

He then makes his own position clear:

“It has thereby become clear that what was mentioned in al-Khulāṣah, that a Rāfiḍī who curses and reviles the two Shaykhs is a disbeliever, is a weak position, contrary to the authoritative texts and commentaries; indeed, it is contrary to the consensus of the jurists, as you have heard.”

(Radd al-Muḥtār, 4:237, Ḥalabī edition.)

And a little further on, he states:

“As for a Rāfiḍī who curses the two Shaykhs, without accusing the Lady ʿĀʾishah of adultery, denying al-Ṣiddīq's companionship [of the Prophet], or anything similar, this is not disbelief, let alone [a case of] not accepting his repentance. Rather, it is misguidance and innovation.”

(Radd al-Muḥtār, 4:237, Ḥalabī edition.)

This is what needs to be understood from his words. The statement of al-Khulāṣah is not, in his view, the position to be relied upon without qualification. He explicitly calls it weak and says that it conflicts with the authoritative texts and commentaries of the madhhab. He further explains in Tanbīh al-Wulāt wa-l-Ḥukkām why the general takfīr of those who insult the Companions is not correct.

He writes there, after discussing the statements of the jurists concerning the acceptance of testimony from the people of divergent doctrines:

“This is the strongest proof that the people of divergent doctrines are not judged to be disbelievers, and likewise the one who abuses the Companions generally. Otherwise, it would not be permissible to accept their reports by which the rulings of the religion are established. Their testimony, however, is not accepted when they openly manifest the abuse, for the reason we have mentioned. Had the one who openly abuses the two Shaykhs or others, on the basis of an interpretation, been a disbeliever, it would not have been valid to explain the rejection of his testimony by his manifest wickedness and his lack of scruple in lying. Rather, it would have been necessary to say that his testimony is not accepted because of his disbelief.”

(Tanbīh al-Wulāt wa-l-Ḥukkām, 1:363, in the passage on the testimony of the people of divergent doctrines.)

This is an important part of his argument. The jurists distinguish between the person whose innovation constitutes disbelief and the person whose innovation does not take him outside Islam. They sometimes reject the testimony of the latter because he openly manifests wickedness, not because he is a disbeliever. Ibn ʿĀbidīn uses this distinction to explain why the mere fact that someone insults the Companions does not establish that he is a disbeliever.

He also quotes the discussion in al-Ikhtiyār concerning the Khārijites and innovators, and then explains the consequence of that discussion:

“In this passage there is a decisive statement that the Khārijites are not disbelievers, and a clear indication that abuse, when based upon an interpretation, even if that interpretation is erroneous, does not constitute disbelief. It also indicates that every one of the Companions is equal in this ruling.”

(Tanbīh al-Wulāt wa-l-Ḥukkām, 1:360; see also the fuller discussion at 1:364.)

He then qualifies the matter, because this does not mean that every doctrine of every innovator is outside the category of disbelief. He continues by explaining that an innovation which contradicts definitive evidence establishing matters of belief and action cannot always be treated as an interpretative excuse. He gives as examples accusing ʿĀʾishah of what Allah declared her innocent of in the Qur'an, denying the companionship of al-Ṣiddīq established by the Qur'an, and claiming that Gabriel made a mistake in the revelation.

This is why he says elsewhere:

“A Rāfiḍī who believes in divinity for ʿAlī, or that Gabriel made a mistake in the revelation, or denies the companionship of al-Ṣiddīq, or accuses the Lady al-Ṣiddīqah [ʿĀʾishah] of adultery, is a disbeliever because he contradicts definitive matters necessarily known to be part of the religion. In contrast, if he prefers ʿAlī or abuses the Companions, he is an innovator, not a disbeliever.”

(Radd al-Muḥtār, 3:46, Ḥalabī edition.)

So the distinction is quite clear. Merely preferring ʿAlī over Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, or abusing the Companions, does not in itself establish disbelief according to Ibn ʿĀbidīn. But where a person holds a doctrine that contradicts definitive teachings of Islam, the ruling is different. In particular, he explicitly states that accusing ʿĀʾishah of adultery is disbelief because it contradicts the Qur'anic declaration of her innocence.

There is also the question of those who declare all the Companions disbelievers. Ibn ʿĀbidīn discusses this separately in Tanbīh al-Wulāt wa-l-Ḥukkām, where he cites Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ's discussion in al-Shifāʾ. He writes that those who declare all the Companions disbelievers must be excepted from the general rule concerning the non-takfīr of the people of innovation, because their doctrine entails repudiating the Qur'anic verses and Prophetic reports establishing the virtue of the Companions.

The example he cites is the Kāmiliyyah among the Rāfiḍah. Their doctrine was not simply that ʿAlī was superior to Abū Bakr, nor merely that some Companions were mistaken. Rather, they declared the entire community after the Prophet (peace and blessings upon him) disbelievers because they did not advance ʿAlī to the caliphate, and even declared ʿAlī a disbeliever for not claiming what they considered his right. Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ explains that this effectively invalidates the transmission of the Qur'an and the Sharīʿah, since those who transmitted them would all be disbelievers according to their claim. Ibn ʿĀbidīn accepts this as an exception to the general treatment of innovators.

(Tanbīh al-Wulāt wa-l-Ḥukkām, 1:364.)

Therefore, the position preferred by Ibn ʿĀbidīn is that the unrestricted statement in al-Khulāṣah concerning the Rāfiḍī who curses the two Shaykhs is weak. Mere Rafḍ, preferring ʿAlī over the two Shaykhs, or abusing the Companions does not, in itself, warrant takfīr. Rather, such conduct is misguidance and innovation. The ruling changes where the person's actual belief or offence contradicts definitive teachings of the religion, as in the explicit examples he gives, including the slander of ʿĀʾishah, denying Abū Bakr's companionship, attributing divinity to ʿAlī, and claiming that Gabriel erred in the revelation.

This is the distinction Ibn ʿĀbidīn makes, and it is important not to confuse the general ruling concerning the people of innovation with the specific ruling concerning those whose doctrines entail disbelief.

And Allah knows best.
