My position is that the Muhammadan Reality is to be understood through a distinction between the primordial inward reality of the Prophet ﷺ and his outward human manifestation in this world. His body, earthly life and human genus are genuinely human; I do not deny his bashariyyah. And, I do not believe that his outward human nature exhausts the reality of his primordial rank and inward spiritual reality.

I understand the Muhammadan Reality as a created Nur associated with his ruh, possessing a unique precedence and rank in the order of creation established by Allah. In this understanding, the Prophet’s ﷺ primordial spiritual reality is distinguished from our own in its rank and particularity, while his bodily manifestation among us remains genuinely human. This distinction does not require denying his humanity or claiming that he is divine. Rather, it recognises that his earthly human manifestation and his uniquely exalted spiritual reality are not identical in every respect.

1. The reports concerning his primordial prophethood
The first report is particularly important because Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾut graded its chain authentic.

Report of Maysarah al-Fajr

The Prophet ﷺ was asked:

**“When were you a prophet?”**

He replied:
**“I was a prophet while Adam was between spirit and body.”**

References: **al-Tahawi, Sharh Mushkil al-Athar, no. 5977, 15:231; Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Musnad, no. 20596, 34:202. Shuʿayb al-Arnaʾut graded its chain sahih.**

مصدر: تخريج مشكل الآثار
A related report from Abu Hurayrah appears in Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi:

**“O Messenger of Allah, when was prophethood established for you?”**

He replied:

**“While Adam was between soul and body.”
Al-Tirmidhi, Jamiʿ, no. 3609, describes the report as hasan sahih gharib.**


These reports establish the extraordinary precedence of the Prophet’s ﷺ prophethood in relation to Adam’s formation. They are relevant to the understanding of his primordial rank, rather than reducing the matter to the ordinary fact that Allah knows the future and reducing it that it means he only knows the future is very dishonest. Ali Qari mentioned this same argument and he also described that using it as only future is wrong.


**To make it work within Ash'ari Framework**:

Al-Sanusi explains that human conditions may occur to the prophets without diminishing their exalted rank. He writes:

**“Illness, for example, may occur to them, but it affects only their outward bodies. As for their hearts, with the knowledge and lights within them whose true measure is known only to our Master … who bestowed them upon them, illness and its like does not diminish them by even the measure of a fingernail.”**

He goes on to explain that such conditions do not cloud the purity of their inward states or diminish their spiritual faculties. 

**Sharh al-Aqidah al-Sughra, Vol. III, pp. 246–247.**

***Even Ashari Giants like him accepted the inward and outward reality distinction.***

This is very important for our Sufi understanding. It does not, by itself, prove that the Prophet’s primordial spiritual reality was the first created Nur, but it gives us an Ashʿari framework for distinguishing bodily humanity from inward spiritual distinction.

Al-Sanusi also uses remarkably elevated language concerning the Prophet’s light in **Sharh al-Aqidah al-Kubra, Volume V, p. 563:**

**“The noblest of creation, whose light is the source of all lights, and in the least of whose light all the lights and forms of knowledge of the saints are drowned.”**

This was in a discussion of the spiritual benefit and superiority associated with meeting the Prophet ﷺ. It demonstrates that exalted language about his light which is not rare in theological writing of Sanusi.

And in In **al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya, Volume I, p. 99**, Ibn ʿArabi identifies the Muhammadan Reality with the Higher Pen in his own cosmological account and calls it something **“originated by Allah from no thing.”**

So this explanation of Nur is fine even within theological frameworks and also works with the Sufi interpretation aswell which is Haqq.
