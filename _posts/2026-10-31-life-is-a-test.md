It's just a test this life , full of suffering at times but this suffering is what makes us better this is not bad or painful for those who understand the divine secrets they accept it with love and embrace them proudly because they know their Lord is testing them and those who have patience and were always grateful then their Lord improves their condition in a way which makes their end and other life beautiful and grants them a great rank among his friends.

Sabr and Adab these two things will take one to Paradise.

Abu Yazid al-Bistami:
I want You, but I do not want You for reward; rather, I want You for punishment.

All my aims have been attained, except the pleasure of finding You in torment.

Ibn Arabi then explains:
As for the person possessing the causes of pain, if he experiences pleasure and delight within himself while those causes ordinarily producing pain remain present, then patience is not obligatory upon him, because he is not a person suffering pain. Rather, he is a person possessing pleasure, moving among bounties from Allah, and he must therefore give thanks for the enjoyment subsisting within him.

He then quotes Umar ibn al-Khattab:
Whenever Allah afflicted me with a calamity, after establishing that I had been afflicted by it, meaning that a calamity, ordinarily a cause producing pain, had descended upon me, I saw that Allah had given me three blessings through that calamity.

The first blessing was that it had not been in my religion. The second was that it had not been greater than it was. The third was the reward Allah promised for it.

I therefore look toward that reward.

Ibn Arabi explains that such a person is not properly described as merely patient because he has recognised several blessings within the very calamity that has befallen him. He must therefore give thanks to Allah.

He continues:
When the hardship of ihram in Hajj reaches you, together with all the painful and burdensome causes it contains, look at the countless blessings Allah has concealed within them. The sight of them will then be followed by enjoyment and pleasure in what you are undertaking, because it is a cause leading you to attain those noble unveilings and tremendous blessings.

Ibn Arabi mentions a conversation with his shaykh, Salih al-Barbari, concerning the inward meaning of adversity.

His shaykh said:
Praise belongs to Allah, the One who bestows in every state. If people knew the flowing grace present in every state, they would not distinguish between prosperity and adversity, and praise would be one.

Ibn Arabi replied:
Rather, it would be made one.

This is pretty interesting because the discussion concerns recognising divine generosity in every state, rather than restricting one's gratitude to favourable circumstances.

The shaykh's point is that the divine bestowal is not absent from adversity. If the servant recognised the grace contained within every state, his praise would not fluctuate according to whether he was experiencing ease or hardship.
