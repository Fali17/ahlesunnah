Shatibi al-Maliki said

**"Ordinary customary matters, insofar as they remain ordinary customs, contain no innovation. But when they are treated as acts of devotion, or placed in the position of devotional practices, innovation enters into them."**

**I'tisam, vol. II, p. 594.**

فالحاصل أن أكثر الحوادث التي أخبر بها النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم من أنها تقع وتظهر وتنتشر أمور مبتدعة على مضاهاة التشريع ، لكن من جهة التعبد ، لا من جهة كونها عادية

He explains that many of the developments foretold by the Prophet (ﷺ), which would occur, appear and spread, are innovations resembling religious legislation, but this is from their devotional aspect, not simply from their customary nature.

His distinction is not between:
- Old customs and new customs.
- Muslim customs and non-Muslim customs.
- Popular customs and unpopular customs.
Rather, his distinction concerns the nature in which the practice is established.

Is it an ordinary human activity, or has it acquired the status of an invented religious observance?

Suppose Muslims organise a children's entertainment event. The children dress up, receive sweets, enjoy games and have fun.

Nobody considers the activity worship.

Nobody believes participating earns religious reward.

Nobody regards it as a sacred occasion.

Nobody participates to honour Christianity, paganism or any religious doctrine.

Under al-Shatibi's distinction, these characteristics fit ordinary entertainment rather than an invented devotional observance.

So calling that activity bid'ah merely because it is new or annually recurring would not be justified as per Shatibi and other major Maliki and Hanafi Imams aswell.
